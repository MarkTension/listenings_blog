Portishead has always been an elusive band. After their ’94 breakthrough with the relatively easygoing, vibey [Dummy](https://open.spotify.com/album/3539EbNgIdEDGBKkUf4wno?si=7ca031143d79414b) (featuring bona fide radio hit [Glory Box](https://open.spotify.com/track/3Ty7OTBNSigGEpeW2PqcsC?si=adad79fecef64eca)) and the [self-titled ’97 follow-up](https://open.spotify.com/album/3G36754KQVLyGuskraEAVA?si=cHRli4RGQrqXndUPQu2R3Q) they disappeared from the radar, only to return in 2008 with the relentless, uncompromising album [Third](https://open.spotify.com/album/4BnNSzOWadogStvyYshJIo?si=ed3N9pKYRmWn5ZQbU0JwZg). This release was followed by a tour that can be seen as a victory march, leaving audiences in awe and introducing the sheer live force of this group to new, young listeners like myself. Back then, I couldn’t find the magic of what I encountered on stage in their first two studio albums, but I did catch it in their ’98 live recording from New York City’s Roseland Ballroom, where the band was accompanied by a 28-piece orchestra. Last month, a remastered version was released – a perfect opportunity to relive this monument of a live album.
