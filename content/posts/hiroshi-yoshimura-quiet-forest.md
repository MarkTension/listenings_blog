About 8 years ago, youtube started recommending to me these 80’s/90’s full-album videos with mysterious Japanese album covers.  
The titles were all Japanese new-age (ambient) music genre, a.k.a. [Kankyo Ongaku](https://kankyongaku.bandcamp.com/album/kanky-ongaku-japanese-ambient-environmental-new-age-music-1980-1990). At the time it was made, Tokyo was rapidly trading nature for expansion, and the music reacted to it by offering a way to ‘touch grass’ at home.  
  
One of my favorites was Hiroshi Yoshimura’s ‘[Green](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jx7CyMZVhHY)’ (1986).  
His work felt really fresh, minimal, and often put me in the right mind-state to read or draw.  
It has has a minimal, video-gamey, utopic sound, where he respects the raw sounds coming from his Yamaha DX7, but he really gets away with it; it’s all interesting, hypnotic and unique.  
The work I wanna highlight today is Quiet Forest, which got released 10 years after Green and was the last album he got out. I never heard about it until the remaster was released exactly this month!  
  
The albums he made in-between Green and this one explore various sounds, but Quiet forest goes kind of Green again, (but (surprisingly) more beat-driven).  
Some songs are more heavy on that new-age-cheesiness (think synthesized pan-flutes), but you gotta love it. Also, the little track ‘After Rain’ deserves some special attention and could be played for hours on repeat in my opinion.  
  
Hope you all enjoy it, and ideally become inspired to loop it while you read.   
  
~ Mark
