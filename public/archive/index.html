Gábor Szabó – 1969

Last week, Spotify bought WhoSampled. For those who do not know, it’s a massive community-driven database of information on reused music. Spotify says it will add WhoSampled’s data to the platform through a forthcoming “SongDNA” feature. The internet’s busiest music nerd, Anthony Fantano, is concerned about the purchase, most importantly because it might give major labels more power to earn money through credits.

The moments I enjoy samples the most are when I stumble upon the original and it turns out to be better— especially when the original is on an album full of beautiful songs. This happened when I heard Gábor Szabó’s “Stormy”, which you’ll instantly recognise from John Legend’s “Save Room”. Gábor himself is not afraid of reusing music either, as most of the songs on “1969” are covers. As can be read on WhoSampled.

Headache - Thank You For Almost Everything

Headache is a project by Vegyn, the elusive producer who became friends with James Blake in a nightclub and ended up working with Frank Ocean on Blonde and Endless. Ever since, he has been releasing some great albums, but this second album under his Headache moniker really is something else. The warm-blooded productions are vintage Vegyn, but what’s new is the spoken word poetry lyrics over it all.

Despite the fact that Headache’s voice is obviously AI-generated, the lyrics are so human and real that this album can really hit the spot. Perfect for listening on headphones in a train while watching the world go by.

Caribou – Honey

I don’t think this is Caribou’s best album, and I didn’t listen to it for a while after hearing the first time. However, after seeing the man on Best Kept Secret this year, I was impressed how well the music works live. Last weekend, a friend gave me the record as a birthday present and I got to revisit it.

Something special happened when I reached the third track ‘Volume’. I got a beautiful flash back to Rainbow Disco Club festival in Japan last year, when Four Tet played it in the beginning of his set. This tune was a great start of a long night full of energy on the dance floor. The recording of Four Tet is on SoundCloud, check it out.

Ryan Davis & the Roadhouse Band - New Threats From The Soul

Last Wednesday, MJ Lenderman & The Wind played a stellar show in Amsterdam. As I suspect many of you are already familiar with his tiptop record Manning Fireworks (if not, go check it out), today’s newsletter is about the equally impressive support act, Ryan Davis & The Roadhouse Band.

As touring becomes more expansive and more of a hassle, it is a great privilege to see a support act bring a six-piece band to the stage, and an excellent one at that. Ryan Davis’ vocals are reminiscent of a less angry Matt Berninger while the band’s sound could be described as Songs: Ohia-inspired with the rhythm section of The War on Drugs. It is a pleasure to hear a band that sounds so comfortable on stage, a vibe they managed to hold on their latest album, recommended here today.

Todd Terje - It's Album Time

This album recommendation might be old for some. Others might only know the hit song ‘Inspector Norse’, which was very popular at parties over ten years ago. ‘It’s Album Time’ (2014) is one of those dance LP’s I often play entirely, as the songs smoothly transition into the next, and have a coherent Space Disco sound. Also, I get reminded of this album a lot when I hear similarities in other music. Like last weekend, at Parallel festival near Barcelona. Denato Dozzy played ‘Apex Break’ by Torn Hawk and Black Deer (2025), and the crazy arps immediately made me think of Terje’s ‘Preben Goes to Acapulco’. When I shared this reference with a friend, he agreed and showed me that in turn Terje’s track was based on ‘Solstice’ by Brian Bennet (1978). I guess we live in a postmodern remake culture, that just keeps on giving. Speaking of remakes, be sure to check out the original version of ‘Johnny and Mary’ by Robert Palmer, which is nicely covered by Bryan Ferry of Roxy Music on ‘It’s Album Time’.
