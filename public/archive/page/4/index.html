Cleo Sol - Lost Angel

I was planning on recommending some serious, lightly sad autumn album to usher in the new season, but this September weekend is giving off very summery vibes here in Amsterdam. That’s why I’ve decided to go with that flow and share this perfect late-summer album with you.

Some of you might recognise Cleo Sol’s voice because she is the most prominent vocalist of the elusive SAULT, a somewhat mysterious collective of musicians helmed by producer Inflo. If you haven’t yet had the pleasure of exploring their work, check out their irresistible track ‘Wildfires’ for a taste.

Loidis - One Day

One Day was released in August, and has succeeded in soundtracking my late-summer.
It’s been hard to miss in the circles where I browse the web. Likely because I’ve been a fan of the artist’s other alias Huerco S, which created this introspective depth-plunging ambient album, For Those Of You Who Have Never (And Also Those Who Have).

On One Day , the ambient makes place for house-y grooves, but the ambient remains in its undercurrents. It has this four-to-the-floor beat, and characteristic house sounds, but those are the only genre-conform aspects of it. Ive read it being called Outsider House.

Don Ray - The Garden of Love

This is my go-to record pick when I’m home alone cooking, cleaning or dancing. It’s just sitting there with its bright coloured square letters, shouting to be pulled out of the cabinet. You might know the opening track Got to Have Loving, if not the original then maybe the highly sought-after edit by Oceanic, for which he took a sample from the amazing 2-minute break halfway the track. However, often I prefer to put the record on my turntable B-side up, starting with the the dripping arpeggio’s of Standing in the Rain, followed by the instant happiness of Garden of Love, ending with the slow but powerful My Desire.

Françoise Hardy - La Question

When Françoise Hardy passed away last month, I must admit my knowledge of her work was limited to her canonical “Tous les garçons et les filles”, first heard in French class when I was 13 years old, and “Comment te dire adieu”.

Shabason, Krgovich, Sage

I haven’t just streamed, but always felt a deep urge to buy almost every release with Joseph Shabason on it. He makes this instrumental, organic, murky, emotionally complex, psychedelic music.
Not so easy to describe, but his signature is his saxophone sound that doesn’t really sound like saxophone at all, and combined with these smooth 90’s keyboard sounds.

Then he had this incredible collaboration ~2 years ago with Nicholas Krgovich, which added vocals to the mix. Camiel and I actually listened to the first single of this record (I’m so happy with my little dog) a bunch on our road trip through the US.

John Talabot - Fin

Two weeks ago on Lentekabinet, I was somehow dissapointed to find out John Talabot had jumped on the trance train that has been riding for a bit too long. Along with DJ’s such as Job Jobse and Young Marco, Talabot is speeding way beyond the 130 limit.

I know I sound like a grumpy old man now…

Luckily, at home I can play the low BPMs of 2011’s release ‘Fin’. A true classic in it’s genre, according to many of my dancefloor friends. It takes me back to the days my feet could still meet the beat and makes me hopeful for a downtempo revival after the superfast music hype is over.

The Kills - Midnight Boom

****

Earlier this month I’ve had the pleasure to see The Kills play Paradiso. Even though the album they released this year was quite decent, I will make the sorry judgment that their creative peak lies in the past. No real shame, as this is how things go for most. After this bad news I do have some good news for them too: their 2008 breakthrough record ‘Midnight Boom’ sounds as cool as ever, showing its first signs of timelessness. After their first two albums being firmly rooted in bluesy and grainy guitar sounds, this record embraces the available technology in a way that clearly sparked creativity while even enhancing their already sizeable natural cool. The deliciously sleazy U.R.A Fever, the upbeat Cheap & Cheerful, the positively shrill Tape Song - true delights. Especially recommended during a bike ride in the dark.

Various Artists – Heisei No Oto (Japanese Left-Field Pop From The CD Age, 1989-1996)

In a few days I’ll be in Japan, together with fellow Listenings-writer Mark. I’m very excited to walk through the largest city on Earth, climb the 100,000 years old Mount Fuji and be surprised by a unique culture.

Evidently I’ve been listening to a lot of great Japanese music lately. The Heisei No Noto compilation by Music from Memory hits the spot for me. It contains many well composed dreamy ambient tracks, that take me to far away places yet to discover. There are also two more beat driven, almost Krautrocky, tunes I especially recommend you to listen to. Pi Po Pa by Yosui Inoue will probably unconsciously make you sing along to the three words of the title, leaving you with a feeling that everything will be alright. On Harai, Keisuke Sakurai sounds like the Japanese brother of Shaggy (you’re right Simon). Mr. Bombastic from Tokyo does a great job mumbling over a fine piano house beat.
