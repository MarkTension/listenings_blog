Sylvie - Sylvie

Sometimes, I just want music to be soothing—no weird noises, no dissonance, just beautiful sounds to ease me through. At the same time, I can be quite critical of soothing music: it should not be too cheesy, too easy, or too sterile. Records that strike that perfect balance are rare. However, for today’s recommendation, Sylvie’s self-titled album, I found one.____

The album was created when Ben Schwab (who also plays in Drugdealer) found some old cassettes in the attic of his parents’ house, containing recordings from his dad’s band in the ’70s. Loving the vibe, he gathered some friends to record an album in the same spirit. Though multiple vocalists appear (Sam Burton, Marina Allen, and Ben’s dad), the album feels like a unified whole, radiating warmth. Soothing in just the right way. Enjoy.

Ike Zwanikken - Greed Gave Me A Lily

Now why I love this album so much might be the same reason as why I’m blown away by David Lynch movies; They never fully disclose the situation. There’s just a deep unknown with different forces at play, which I’ll never understand. I’m just somewhere on the surface of it, blissfully being pulled along, meandering between scary / loving / funny.

Not that it’s the same vibe, but this album does the same thing to me. It’s warm, nostalgic, weirdly alien, and then loving. Just new worlds to explore. There’s nothing really like it.

Black Devil Disco Club - Black Devil Disco Club

During Christmas, a dinner at a friend’s place ended in dancing in the kitchen and eventually moving to the club for a long night out. One thing that got us going was the record player in that kitchen and the extensive vinyl collection of the owner.

The record that did it for me, and which I played again during the hangover, was Black Devil Disco Club’s first release from 1978. This six-track EP is a contiguous trip down the depths of disco hell. You get taken along on a kick-snare ride with both refreshing and disturbing synthesizer noises and echoing sing-alongs. I was surprised to find out this record is from the 70’s, as many of the sounds can be still be heard in modern dance music today.

Gena Rose Bruce - Deep Is The Way

For this weekend’s pick, we venture all the way out to the land down under. Although Gena Rose Bruce is technically a singer-songwriter, this album has too much grit and schwung to be classified as another “girl-with-guitar”-record.

Hailing from Melbourne, she made the excellent “Can’t Make You Love Me” in 2019, an album that somehow managed to grab the attention of Bill Callahan, resulting in a gorgeous duet on this week’s recommendation, “Deep Is The Way”. Perfect for Sundays.

Mr YT - Brand New Day

Today’s listening is Mr YT (Yuji Takenouchi)’s Brand New Day.

The first song, ‘Morning’, is just unbelievable. Such a journey. ’Nite’ is a close second. For both it’s quite hard to put a finger on which time they really belongs in. Could be because Takenouchi scored some iconic and futuristic video games for Sony, which must’ve conjured up otherwordly scenes in his mind on a daily basis.

The other songs are cool housey danceable tunes, which hint at the album’s creation time (1997) way better. Also very cool.

Discovery Zone - Remote Control

Do you remember the early days of the internet, when surfing the web sounded like visiting a parallel universe and AI was seen as something with supernatural powers? That’s the vibe I get from listening to JJ Weihl (New York) aka Discovery Zone. Last week I went to her concert in Cinetol, together with fellow Listenings writer Mark, and dear Listenings listener Isa. The setup was amazing, with Discovery Zone standing behind a see-through screen on which nostalgic cyberspace animations were beamed throughout the show. Imagine flying dolphins, geometrical pulsating patterns and lively color streams. A well designed mix between irony and beauty. The whole experience left me feeling excited for a time to come yet already passed.

Songs: Ohia - Magnolia Electric Co.

Early this summer, I found myself driving along the endless but gorgeous roads of the Scottish coast, exploring the country’s outer isles. Thanks to its fickle weather, Scotland is no destination for the average sun-and-beach vacation—it actually felt like fall had already arrived.

One day, when the sun was shining but rain clouds were starting to roll in, our road trip soundtrack needed an adjustment. We chose Magnolia Electric Co. (2003) by the late Jason Molina, released under his Songs: Ohia moniker. This dark, compelling album has a rare clarity and beauty, recorded live in the studio so it almost feels like you’re in the audience at an intense show. It’s perfect for a rainy day—just like the ones we are about to see plenty of.

M. Sage - The Wind of Things

M. Sage’s The Wind Of Things is an album dear to my heart.

I once used it as a soundtrack when reading John Steinbeck’s Travels With Charley , which is a heartwarming story of John and his dog Charley road-tripping the US in the 60’s. It pairs so well because The Wind of Things is heartwarming as well.

The music is a series of ambient soundscapes. And when you think of ambient you can imagine lushy synthesizers and over-exaggerated reverb exploring the ether. That’s not the case here though.

John Maus - We Must Become the Pitiless Censors of Ourselves

Last week I went to Paradiso in Amsterdam to see John Maus for the first time. I had not listened to his music since the release of his album ‘We Must Become the Pitiless Censors of Ourselves’ in 2011, and I had no idea what to expect from the show.

When support act Pol finished and took their guitars, drums and synthesisers, all that was left was a laptop in the middle of the stage. Soon John walked up and kneeled in front of the computer, turning his back to the audience, and hit the play button. He then jumped up and went raving and running like a maniac across the stage, shouting into the microphone over the prerecorded music, inviting the audience to shout along. The scene reminded me of a pumped up priest spreading God’s energy in a church. This thing repeated every time he put on the next song, until he was soaking wet from sweat.
