Foxwarren - 2

“Foxwarren” is a small town in western Manitoba, Canada and although I would have loved to tell you all about the interesting stuff I’ve encountered on my recent Streetview exploration, this is a music newsletter and Foxwarren is also a band.

It’s the band that was already fronted by singer Andy Shauf before his excellent 2016 breakthrough solo album “The Party”, and I’m glad that despite Shauf his solo success, the band still put out their debut album in 2018. I am even more delighted that this album has received a successor this year.

Skee Mask - Compro

Skee Mask is known for a bunch of amazing releases on Ilian Tape. This is his second album, and it’s incredible.

The cover says a lot; Misty, obscured, and deep. It’s easy to get lost in the sauce with the lush atmospheric sounds. The beats keep things tightly together though. It has a sort of dub-techno, ambient techno feel, but at the same time this meticulous hip-hop style chopped-up beats give it some real magic.

Wilson Tanner - Legends

While writing this, I’m in that hazy state of happiness and tiredness, as it’s the Monday after three days of Best Kept Secret festival. I’m re-watching the concert registrations on my phone, while lying in sun. Little kids are jumping in the water next to my house, and I’m about to follow. The soundtrack that fits my mood is the latest album of Wilson Tanner. You might know one of the duo, under the name of Andras. Together the two Aussies create peaceful ambient, that makes you feel like being on a vineyard in rural Perth.

Amen Dunes - Freedom

Today, on May 5th, we celebrate freedom in The Netherlands. Exactly 80 years ago the German occupation ended.

Last week in Scotland I was reminded of the country’s long fight for independence, when I learned about a statue of William Wallace from the movie Braveheart, as played by Mel Gibson, in the city of Stirling. The word ‘Freedom’ is written on the base of the statue, referring to the famous quote “they may take our lives, but they’ll never take our freedom”. Because the thing is pretty ugly, and has the face of Mel Gibson, the Scots ridiculed and vandalised it. To protect it, a fence was placed around it. The result: an encased Braveheart figure standing for freedom. Oh, the irony.

Saya Gray - SAYA

Now that the sunny weather seems to have left us, it is time to bring back those vibes with Saya Gray’s deliciously breezy, yet intricately layered latest album, released last February. The melodies are both meticulous and meandering and fit in perfectly with the crisp and layered production. It’s poppy, organic, clever and effortless at the same time.

I may seem a bit over-generous with the adjectives, but I believe they are justified. In the first track, Saya tells us she doesn’t fall in love in the spring time - easier said than done when, on this album, there’s so much to fall for.

Letta Mbulu - In The Music The Village Never Ends

About ten years ago during Afro heyday, virtually every DJ in Amsterdam carried the down-tempo ‘Normalizo’ of this album. I heard it so often, I couldn’t listen to the South African Letta Mbulu for a long while. However, recently I started playing the album again because of the last track, ‘The Village’. It takes me back to a memorable moment at the end of a beautiful night in the basement of De School. At around 6 in the morning, after an hour of spacey twisted techno, this uplifting call-and-response tune woke me up and let me leave the club with a big smiling face.

man oh man! - Martin Luke Brown

For today’s recommendation, we go back in time sonically to the early 2010s, when the indie scene was dominated by the subdued, lo-fi sounds of Youth Lagoon, Unknown Mortal Orchestra and Mac DeMarco. Nowadays, such sounds are hard to come by. For those of you with some nostalgia towards this era I, I found an English Gen Z’er who completely embraced this vibe and wrote some stellar songs basking in 2010s glory. Unoriginal? Maybe. But there definitely is enough freshness in these songs, and yesterday I found out they work extremely well when listening when spring is in the air.

Il Quadro di Troisi - Il Quadro di Troisi

Il Quadro di Troisi is Eva Geist and Denato Dozzy. Two Italian producers with a fine taste; just look at them lounging in the curved couch on the cover of their self-titled debut album (2020). A combination that results in dreamy vocals and trippy arpeggiators. The tracks are very much loop based, just like Dozzy’s solo techno productions. But slower, and structured like pop songs. At times, the album even includes classic Italo Disco elements. For instance the bass of ‘Raggio Verde’.
