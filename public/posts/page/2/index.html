Ryan Davis & the Roadhouse Band - New Threats From The Soul

Last Wednesday, MJ Lenderman & The Wind played a stellar show in Amsterdam. As I suspect many of you are already familiar with his tiptop record Manning Fireworks (if not, go check it out), today’s newsletter is about the equally impressive support act, Ryan Davis & The Roadhouse Band.

As touring becomes more expansive and more of a hassle, it is a great privilege to see a support act bring a six-piece band to the stage, and an excellent one at that. Ryan Davis’ vocals are reminiscent of a less angry Matt Berninger while the band’s sound could be described as Songs: Ohia-inspired with the rhythm section of The War on Drugs. It is a pleasure to hear a band that sounds so comfortable on stage, a vibe they managed to hold on their latest album, recommended here today.

Todd Terje - It's Album Time

This album recommendation might be old for some. Others might only know the hit song ‘Inspector Norse’, which was very popular at parties over ten years ago. ‘It’s Album Time’ (2014) is one of those dance LP’s I often play entirely, as the songs smoothly transition into the next, and have a coherent Space Disco sound. Also, I get reminded of this album a lot when I hear similarities in other music. Like last weekend, at Parallel festival near Barcelona. Denato Dozzy played ‘Apex Break’ by Torn Hawk and Black Deer (2025), and the crazy arps immediately made me think of Terje’s ‘Preben Goes to Acapulco’. When I shared this reference with a friend, he agreed and showed me that in turn Terje’s track was based on ‘Solstice’ by Brian Bennet (1978). I guess we live in a postmodern remake culture, that just keeps on giving. Speaking of remakes, be sure to check out the original version of ‘Johnny and Mary’ by Robert Palmer, which is nicely covered by Bryan Ferry of Roxy Music on ‘It’s Album Time’.

Skee Mask - Compro

Skee Mask is known for a bunch of amazing releases on Ilian Tape. This is his second album, and it’s incredible.

The cover says a lot; Misty, obscured, and deep. It’s easy to get lost in the sauce with the lush atmospheric sounds. The beats keep things tightly together though. It has a sort of dub-techno, ambient techno feel, but at the same time this meticulous hip-hop style chopped-up beats give it some real magic.

Amen Dunes - Freedom

Today, on May 5th, we celebrate freedom in The Netherlands. Exactly 80 years ago the German occupation ended.

Last week in Scotland I was reminded of the country’s long fight for independence, when I learned about a statue of William Wallace from the movie Braveheart, as played by Mel Gibson, in the city of Stirling. The word ‘Freedom’ is written on the base of the statue, referring to the famous quote “they may take our lives, but they’ll never take our freedom”. Because the thing is pretty ugly, and has the face of Mel Gibson, the Scots ridiculed and vandalised it. To protect it, a fence was placed around it. The result: an encased Braveheart figure standing for freedom. Oh, the irony.

Saya Gray - SAYA

Now that the sunny weather seems to have left us, it is time to bring back those vibes with Saya Gray’s deliciously breezy, yet intricately layered latest album, released last February. The melodies are both meticulous and meandering and fit in perfectly with the crisp and layered production. It’s poppy, organic, clever and effortless at the same time.

I may seem a bit over-generous with the adjectives, but I believe they are justified. In the first track, Saya tells us she doesn’t fall in love in the spring time - easier said than done when, on this album, there’s so much to fall for.

Only when the album finished I realized what a deep trip I was in. Listening to music without knowing that I was, was like my head-space made it by itself.
