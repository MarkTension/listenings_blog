Chaos in the CBD - Midnight in Peckham

No, CBD does not refer to the stuff you get at the smart shop. It means Central Business District, a term commonly used in New Zeeland (and Australia) when talking about a city center. The two brothers in Chaos in the CBD hail from Auckland, and moved to London shortly after the duo was formed. As for the title, Peckham is a neighbourhood in London.

Having that out of the way, let’s talk music. I bought this record for a good friend in Scotland, at a lovely dance music store in Glasgow called Rub-A-Dub. I picked it because it’s dance music that is great for listening at home. It’s easy on the ear, but never boring. The chords and melodies are jazzy and soothing, but combined with off beat rhythms they become hypnotising. It uses cliché in a way that turns out interesting — the title song could have been a corny brass house tune, but accompanied with lo-fi drums it sounds convincingly different. Ten years after its release, Midnight in Peckham still hits the spot for me.

Gábor Szabó – 1969

Last week, Spotify bought WhoSampled. For those who do not know, it’s a massive community-driven database of information on reused music. Spotify says it will add WhoSampled’s data to the platform through a forthcoming “SongDNA” feature. The internet’s busiest music nerd, Anthony Fantano, is concerned about the purchase, most importantly because it might give major labels more power to earn money through credits.

The moments I enjoy samples the most are when I stumble upon the original and it turns out to be better— especially when the original is on an album full of beautiful songs. This happened when I heard Gábor Szabó’s “Stormy”, which you’ll instantly recognise from John Legend’s “Save Room”. Gábor himself is not afraid of reusing music either, as most of the songs on “1969” are covers. As can be read on WhoSampled.

Caribou – Honey

I don’t think this is Caribou’s best album, and I didn’t listen to it for a while after hearing the first time. However, after seeing the man on Best Kept Secret this year, I was impressed how well the music works live. Last weekend, a friend gave me the record as a birthday present and I got to revisit it.

Something special happened when I reached the third track ‘Volume’. I got a beautiful flash back to Rainbow Disco Club festival in Japan last year, when Four Tet played it in the beginning of his set. This tune was a great start of a long night full of energy on the dance floor. The recording of Four Tet is on SoundCloud, check it out.

Todd Terje - It's Album Time

This album recommendation might be old for some. Others might only know the hit song ‘Inspector Norse’, which was very popular at parties over ten years ago. ‘It’s Album Time’ (2014) is one of those dance LP’s I often play entirely, as the songs smoothly transition into the next, and have a coherent Space Disco sound. Also, I get reminded of this album a lot when I hear similarities in other music. Like last weekend, at Parallel festival near Barcelona. Denato Dozzy played ‘Apex Break’ by Torn Hawk and Black Deer (2025), and the crazy arps immediately made me think of Terje’s ‘Preben Goes to Acapulco’. When I shared this reference with a friend, he agreed and showed me that in turn Terje’s track was based on ‘Solstice’ by Brian Bennet (1978). I guess we live in a postmodern remake culture, that just keeps on giving. Speaking of remakes, be sure to check out the original version of ‘Johnny and Mary’ by Robert Palmer, which is nicely covered by Bryan Ferry of Roxy Music on ‘It’s Album Time’.

Amen Dunes - Freedom

Today, on May 5th, we celebrate freedom in The Netherlands. Exactly 80 years ago the German occupation ended.

Last week in Scotland I was reminded of the country’s long fight for independence, when I learned about a statue of William Wallace from the movie Braveheart, as played by Mel Gibson, in the city of Stirling. The word ‘Freedom’ is written on the base of the statue, referring to the famous quote “they may take our lives, but they’ll never take our freedom”. Because the thing is pretty ugly, and has the face of Mel Gibson, the Scots ridiculed and vandalised it. To protect it, a fence was placed around it. The result: an encased Braveheart figure standing for freedom. Oh, the irony.

Letta Mbulu - In The Music The Village Never Ends

About ten years ago during Afro heyday, virtually every DJ in Amsterdam carried the down-tempo ‘Normalizo’ of this album. I heard it so often, I couldn’t listen to the South African Letta Mbulu for a long while. However, recently I started playing the album again because of the last track, ‘The Village’. It takes me back to a memorable moment at the end of a beautiful night in the basement of De School. At around 6 in the morning, after an hour of spacey twisted techno, this uplifting call-and-response tune woke me up and let me leave the club with a big smiling face.

Black Devil Disco Club - Black Devil Disco Club

During Christmas, a dinner at a friend’s place ended in dancing in the kitchen and eventually moving to the club for a long night out. One thing that got us going was the record player in that kitchen and the extensive vinyl collection of the owner.

The record that did it for me, and which I played again during the hangover, was Black Devil Disco Club’s first release from 1978. This six-track EP is a contiguous trip down the depths of disco hell. You get taken along on a kick-snare ride with both refreshing and disturbing synthesizer noises and echoing sing-alongs. I was surprised to find out this record is from the 70’s, as many of the sounds can be still be heard in modern dance music today.

Discovery Zone - Remote Control

Do you remember the early days of the internet, when surfing the web sounded like visiting a parallel universe and AI was seen as something with supernatural powers? That’s the vibe I get from listening to JJ Weihl (New York) aka Discovery Zone. Last week I went to her concert in Cinetol, together with fellow Listenings writer Mark, and dear Listenings listener Isa. The setup was amazing, with Discovery Zone standing behind a see-through screen on which nostalgic cyberspace animations were beamed throughout the show. Imagine flying dolphins, geometrical pulsating patterns and lively color streams. A well designed mix between irony and beauty. The whole experience left me feeling excited for a time to come yet already passed.

John Maus - We Must Become the Pitiless Censors of Ourselves

Last week I went to Paradiso in Amsterdam to see John Maus for the first time. I had not listened to his music since the release of his album ‘We Must Become the Pitiless Censors of Ourselves’ in 2011, and I had no idea what to expect from the show.

When support act Pol finished and took their guitars, drums and synthesisers, all that was left was a laptop in the middle of the stage. Soon John walked up and kneeled in front of the computer, turning his back to the audience, and hit the play button. He then jumped up and went raving and running like a maniac across the stage, shouting into the microphone over the prerecorded music, inviting the audience to shout along. The scene reminded me of a pumped up priest spreading God’s energy in a church. This thing repeated every time he put on the next song, until he was soaking wet from sweat.
