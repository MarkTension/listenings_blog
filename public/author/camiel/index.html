Headache - Thank You For Almost Everything

Headache is a project by Vegyn, the elusive producer who became friends with James Blake in a nightclub and ended up working with Frank Ocean on Blonde and Endless. Ever since, he has been releasing some great albums, but this second album under his Headache moniker really is something else. The warm-blooded productions are vintage Vegyn, but what’s new is the spoken word poetry lyrics over it all.

Despite the fact that Headache’s voice is obviously AI-generated, the lyrics are so human and real that this album can really hit the spot. Perfect for listening on headphones in a train while watching the world go by.

Ryan Davis & the Roadhouse Band - New Threats From The Soul

Last Wednesday, MJ Lenderman & The Wind played a stellar show in Amsterdam. As I suspect many of you are already familiar with his tiptop record Manning Fireworks (if not, go check it out), today’s newsletter is about the equally impressive support act, Ryan Davis & The Roadhouse Band.

As touring becomes more expansive and more of a hassle, it is a great privilege to see a support act bring a six-piece band to the stage, and an excellent one at that. Ryan Davis’ vocals are reminiscent of a less angry Matt Berninger while the band’s sound could be described as Songs: Ohia-inspired with the rhythm section of The War on Drugs. It is a pleasure to hear a band that sounds so comfortable on stage, a vibe they managed to hold on their latest album, recommended here today.

Saya Gray - SAYA

Now that the sunny weather seems to have left us, it is time to bring back those vibes with Saya Gray’s deliciously breezy, yet intricately layered latest album, released last February. The melodies are both meticulous and meandering and fit in perfectly with the crisp and layered production. It’s poppy, organic, clever and effortless at the same time.

I may seem a bit over-generous with the adjectives, but I believe they are justified. In the first track, Saya tells us she doesn’t fall in love in the spring time - easier said than done when, on this album, there’s so much to fall for.

Sylvie - Sylvie

Sometimes, I just want music to be soothing—no weird noises, no dissonance, just beautiful sounds to ease me through. At the same time, I can be quite critical of soothing music: it should not be too cheesy, too easy, or too sterile. Records that strike that perfect balance are rare. However, for today’s recommendation, Sylvie’s self-titled album, I found one.____

The album was created when Ben Schwab (who also plays in Drugdealer) found some old cassettes in the attic of his parents’ house, containing recordings from his dad’s band in the ’70s. Loving the vibe, he gathered some friends to record an album in the same spirit. Though multiple vocalists appear (Sam Burton, Marina Allen, and Ben’s dad), the album feels like a unified whole, radiating warmth. Soothing in just the right way. Enjoy.

Songs: Ohia - Magnolia Electric Co.

Early this summer, I found myself driving along the endless but gorgeous roads of the Scottish coast, exploring the country’s outer isles. Thanks to its fickle weather, Scotland is no destination for the average sun-and-beach vacation—it actually felt like fall had already arrived.

One day, when the sun was shining but rain clouds were starting to roll in, our road trip soundtrack needed an adjustment. We chose Magnolia Electric Co. (2003) by the late Jason Molina, released under his Songs: Ohia moniker. This dark, compelling album has a rare clarity and beauty, recorded live in the studio so it almost feels like you’re in the audience at an intense show. It’s perfect for a rainy day—just like the ones we are about to see plenty of.
