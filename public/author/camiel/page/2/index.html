Cleo Sol - Lost Angel

I was planning on recommending some serious, lightly sad autumn album to usher in the new season, but this September weekend is giving off very summery vibes here in Amsterdam. That’s why I’ve decided to go with that flow and share this perfect late-summer album with you.

Some of you might recognise Cleo Sol’s voice because she is the most prominent vocalist of the elusive SAULT, a somewhat mysterious collective of musicians helmed by producer Inflo. If you haven’t yet had the pleasure of exploring their work, check out their irresistible track ‘Wildfires’ for a taste.

Françoise Hardy - La Question

When Françoise Hardy passed away last month, I must admit my knowledge of her work was limited to her canonical “Tous les garçons et les filles”, first heard in French class when I was 13 years old, and “Comment te dire adieu”.

As much as I appreciated these two airy songs evoking sepia images of a pre-tourism-boom Paris, I had no idea about the cultural context that gave rise to these songs and their counterparts like France Gall’s “Poupee de cire poupee de son”.

The Kills - Midnight Boom

****

Earlier this month I’ve had the pleasure to see The Kills play Paradiso. Even though the album they released this year was quite decent, I will make the sorry judgment that their creative peak lies in the past. No real shame, as this is how things go for most. After this bad news I do have some good news for them too: their 2008 breakthrough record ‘Midnight Boom’ sounds as cool as ever, showing its first signs of timelessness. After their first two albums being firmly rooted in bluesy and grainy guitar sounds, this record embraces the available technology in a way that clearly sparked creativity while even enhancing their already sizeable natural cool. The deliciously sleazy U.R.A Fever, the upbeat Cheap & Cheerful, the positively shrill Tape Song - true delights. Especially recommended during a bike ride in the dark.

Portishead - Roseland NYC live

Portishead has always been an elusive band. After their ’94 breakthrough with the relatively easygoing, vibey Dummy (featuring bona fide radio hit Glory Box) and the self-titled ’97 follow-up they disappeared from the radar, only to return in 2008 with the relentless, uncompromising album Third. This release was followed by a tour that can be seen as a victory march, leaving audiences in awe and introducing the sheer live force of this group to new, young listeners like myself. Back then, I couldn’t find the magic of what I encountered on stage in their first two studio albums, but I did catch it in their ’98 live recording from New York City’s Roseland Ballroom, where the band was accompanied by a 28-piece orchestra. Last month, a remastered version was released – a perfect opportunity to relive this monument of a live album.

Linda Perhacs – Parallelograms (1970)

Spotify, Bandcamp

At its western edge, Los Angeles borders the rugged hills of Topanga State Park. In the 1920s Hollywood stars found refuge in the quaint canyon. The 60s brought in the creatives of that time - Neil Young wrote most of his After the Goldrush there. Another resident those days was Linda Perhacs, a twenty something Californian working as a dental hygienist in Beverly Hills, writing songs in Topanga in her spare time.
