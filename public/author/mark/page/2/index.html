M. Sage - The Wind of Things

M. Sage’s The Wind Of Things is an album dear to my heart.

I once used it as a soundtrack when reading John Steinbeck’s Travels With Charley , which is a heartwarming story of John and his dog Charley road-tripping the US in the 60’s. It pairs so well because The Wind of Things is heartwarming as well.

The music is a series of ambient soundscapes. And when you think of ambient you can imagine lushy synthesizers and over-exaggerated reverb exploring the ether. That’s not the case here though.

Loidis - One Day

One Day was released in August, and has succeeded in soundtracking my late-summer.
It’s been hard to miss in the circles where I browse the web. Likely because I’ve been a fan of the artist’s other alias Huerco S, which created this introspective depth-plunging ambient album, For Those Of You Who Have Never (And Also Those Who Have).

On One Day , the ambient makes place for house-y grooves, but the ambient remains in its undercurrents. It has this four-to-the-floor beat, and characteristic house sounds, but those are the only genre-conform aspects of it. Ive read it being called Outsider House.

Shabason, Krgovich, Sage

I haven’t just streamed, but always felt a deep urge to buy almost every release with Joseph Shabason on it. He makes this instrumental, organic, murky, emotionally complex, psychedelic music.
Not so easy to describe, but his signature is his saxophone sound that doesn’t really sound like saxophone at all, and combined with these smooth 90’s keyboard sounds.

Then he had this incredible collaboration ~2 years ago with Nicholas Krgovich, which added vocals to the mix. Camiel and I actually listened to the first single of this record (I’m so happy with my little dog) a bunch on our road trip through the US.

Shinetiac - Not All Who Wander Are Lost

Lately I’ve been listening a lot to releases by the Kansas label West Mineral Ltd.

Initially discovered through Purelink, featured last time by me, who shared some of their inspirations in an interview.

The label’s been releasing some real top-notch psychedelic, experimental, electronic music, ranging from ambient to more beat-driven energy levels. Very interesting, complex, and inspiring to my own music productions as well.

The release I wanted to highlight today by Shinetiac, Not all Who Wander Are Lost, has been quite dear to me in the last 2 months. It’s hard to pinpoint what exact mood it conveys. At some points it’s deeply soothing, but never fully, sometimes feeling lost, while being kind of nostalgic or sad at the same time. It’s really quite complex when you listen deeply, a real treat for the brain, so alienating that I feel human.
